What happens to your body if you do a glute bridge every single day for thirty days?
It looks easy. Lie on your back, push your hips up, and hold. Easy… until second twenty.
That burn? That's your glutes. One of the biggest muscles in your body, and for most of us, the laziest, because we spend all day sitting on it.
Week one: you hold for thirty seconds. Your hamstrings start shaking, and you realize how weak your glutes really were.
Here's the trick: squeeze your glutes at the top, keep your ribs down, and don't arch your lower back. Your hips do the work, not your spine.
Week two: forty-five seconds. Your core switches on to keep your body in a straight line, from your knees to your shoulders.
Week three: one full minute. Climbing stairs feels lighter. Getting up from a chair feels easier. Your hips feel more stable when you walk.
Week four: you lift one leg. Single-leg bridge. Now each side has to hold your hips level on its own… and you find out which side was doing less.
But here's what most people don't expect.
When your glutes are weak, your lower back picks up the slack. Research shows that strengthening your hips and glutes, along with your core, can help reduce lower back pain.
So if you sit all day, this one is for you.
