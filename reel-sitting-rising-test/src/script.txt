Can you sit on the floor and stand back up without using your hands? This simple test may say more about your health than you think.
It's called the sitting-rising test. Cross your feet, sit down on the floor, then stand back up.
You start with ten points: five to sit down, five to stand up. Every time you use a hand, a knee or an arm for support, you lose one point. Lose your balance? Half a point.
In a study of more than two thousand adults aged fifty-one to eighty, people with the lowest scores were much more likely to die over the next six years than people who scored eight or higher.
That doesn't mean the test causes anything. It shows your strength, balance and flexibility working together.
The good news? You can train all three.
